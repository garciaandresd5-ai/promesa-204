# Institutions and incentives

The lens I keep coming back to: outcomes follow from the rules and from who has agency inside them.

## Where I've used it
- [[FDI in the Caribbean]] argued that institutional agency, not the incentive itself, drives developmental outcomes.
- [[Marriage collapse in Puerto Rico]] ended on a falsifiable institutional mechanism instead of a cultural story.
- [[Proyecto Esencia and Act 60]] produced a classical liberal reform framework.
- [[PROMESA Section 204 count]] reads litigation spending as a costly signal of sincerity.
- [[SSDI and prime age men]] asks whether a program's design pulls men out of work.
- [[Boston short term rentals]] tests what a city ordinance actually changed.

## Thinkers in the mix
[[Hayek]], [[Acemoglu]], [[Nicholas Eberstadt]]

## Questions to write toward
- Where do my classical liberal reform ideas and Acemoglu's institutions argument agree, and where do they split?
- The costly signal idea from the PROMESA count: what other policy fights could it explain?
